Concept 29 — Emerging

AI-Native Interface

Not a look — a interaction grammar. Where Baseline X describes what AI-assisted code tends to look like, this describes what AI-driven products behave like: a prompt instead of a form, answers that stream in token by token, visible tool calls, inline citations, and skeleton states standing in for content that does not exist yet.

  • • Latency is designed for, not hidden — streaming replaces spinners.
  • • The model's work is shown: tool calls and sources are surfaced, not buried.
  • • Output is non-deterministic, so every surface needs a correction path.

Concept 32 — Classic

Bauhaus

The 1919 school that produced the grammar everything after it borrowed. Form follows function; ornament is stripped to the primary triad — red, blue, yellow — and the three primitives: circle, square, triangle.

Concept H (Swiss) is its direct descendant, but Bauhaus is the warmer ancestor: it keeps saturated colour and playful geometry where Swiss went monochrome and strictly typographic.

Concept 33 — Classic

Art Deco

The luxury language of 1925: strict bilateral symmetry, stepped and faceted forms, sunburst and chevron motifs, and gold on deep lacquer. It reads as expensive because every line is deliberate and nothing is accidental.

Still the default for spirits, hotels, jewellery and title sequences — any brand that needs to signal craft and permanence rather than speed.

Concept 35 — Practical

Editorial & Long-Form

Reading is the one task where the interface should disappear entirely, and the rules for it were settled centuries before the browser. A measure of roughly sixty-five characters, leading near 1.75, a serif sized for sustained reading, and generous space between paragraphs will outperform any amount of visual invention.

What makes this a design concept rather than a default is the discipline it demands. Every element that is not the text — sidebars, related links, sticky bars, newsletter modals — competes with the only thing the reader came for.

The measure is the design. Everything else is arrangement.

Used by publications, documentation, and any product whose value is carried in prose. It is the direct opposite of Concept G: where maximalism competes for attention, editorial protects it.

On the measure: 65ch is set on the container rather than the body text, so it holds at any font size the reader chooses — a fixed pixel width would break the moment they zoom.

Concept 37 — Movements

Paris · 1896

Jugendstil

Art Nouveau · after Mucha

Ornament here is not applied to a layout — it is the layout. The arch defines the panel, the whiplash line carries the eye between blocks, and the hairline frame does the work a border-radius would do in any other concept. Remove the ornament and there is no composition left underneath it.

The palette is deliberately tertiary — ivory, ochre, sage, faded rose — because the style's contrast comes from line weight, not from hue. That is also its cost: at hairline weights on cream, the gold sits close to the contrast floor, so it may carry structure but never body text.

Three restrictions and nothing else: only horizontals and verticals, only the three primaries plus black, white and grey, and never symmetry. Balance is achieved by weight rather than by mirroring — a small red block far from centre answers a large white one near it.

It is the most constrained language on this page, and the constraint is what makes it instantly legible. Add a diagonal, a curve or a fourth hue and the whole thing reads as generic modernism.

Concept 40 — Movements

Neon Rose

Psychedelic · after Moscoso · SF 1967

The whole style rests on one perceptual trick: place two saturated complementaries at the same luminance and the eye cannot resolve the edge between them, so it appears to shimmer. Magenta against lime does this; magenta against white does not.

Legibility is spent on purpose. A concert poster only has to be readable long enough to make you walk over to it, and the difficulty was the invitation.

On a screen that trade is far more expensive than it was on a wall. Confine the vibration to display type, keep body copy on this flat panel, and the effect survives contact with Concept 34.

Weingart taught inside the Swiss school and then spent a career pulling it apart. Every constraint in this style is a hardware fact. Eight colours because the signal carried three bits. Blocky mosaics because the character ROM had no curves. Double-height headlines because a control code could stretch a row, and nothing finer existed.

That is exactly why it still works as a design language: the restrictions were never taste, so they never dated. Snap everything to the character cell, refuse tints and blends, and the result is coherent without a single judgement call.

The trap is the palette. Pure blue on black and pure red on black both fail body-size contrast, and the original medium got away with it at a viewing distance no monitor has. Keep white, yellow and cyan for anything that must be read.

The demoscene took the same character-cell constraint as Concept 42 and drew pictures with it anyway. Gradients are three shade glyphs — light, medium, dark — because there was no alpha to fade with, and the whole palette is the sixteen EGA colours on a blue field nobody chose but everybody kept.

EXPERIMENTAL · 10 SPECIMENS

The Voyage

Every concept above is a bundle of variables that happen to travel together. Pull one out, change it, and you find out which ones were actually doing the work — and which were just along for the ride.

Ten dissections follow. Four succeeded outright, four work only inside a narrow band, two failed. The failures are kept in full, because a mutation that dies tells you exactly where the boundary of the parent concept is.

EXP-01 · PARENT: B GLASSMORPHISM
Knockout Glass
PARTIAL
Dissection

Glass is four variables: a vivid backdrop, a translucent panel, a blur, and opaque type on top. The panel is the figure; the backdrop is the ground.

Mutation

Swap figure and ground. Make the panel fully opaque and cut the type out of it, so the vivid layer is visible only through the letterforms.

Result

At display size the letterforms are wide enough to carry the gradient and the effect is striking — the type becomes the window. At body size it collapses: the stems are too narrow to read as an aperture, and contrast falls below usable. The specimen shows both sizes so the failure point is visible.

APERTURE
SAME TREATMENT, BODY SIZE — FAILS

At this size the letterform stems are narrower than the gradient's features, so the type stops reading as a window and starts reading as low-contrast noise.

EXP-02 · PARENT: H SWISS MINIMALISM
The Weight Grid
PARTIAL
Dissection

Swiss encodes hierarchy in size, position and space. Weight is barely used — historically because metal type offered two or three weights, not nine.

Mutation

Delete size variation entirely. One size for everything; hierarchy carried only by a variable font's weight axis, 100 → 900.

Result

Excellent for dense indexes and tables of contents — the ragged silhouette of mixed sizes disappears and the column edge goes perfectly clean. But weight is a far weaker signal than size at distance: squint and the hierarchy vanishes, where a size hierarchy survives. Usable for scanning a list, not for orienting on a page.

EXP-03 · PARENTS: A NEOBRUTALISM × D NEUMORPHISM
Hard Soft
FAILED
Dissection

Both concepts are built almost entirely from box-shadow, but they use it to assert opposite physics. Neobrutalism: large offset, zero blur, one hard light. Neumorphism: paired offsets, heavy blur, diffuse ambient light.

Mutation

Run both shadow systems on the same element and see whether the tactility compounds.

Result

It does not. The offset shadow claims a hard object under a point light; the blurred pair claims a soft form under ambient light. The eye cannot resolve both, so it resolves neither — the result reads as a rendering mistake rather than a material. Finding: blur radius and offset are not independent design variables. They jointly declare a lighting model, and a surface may only have one.

EXP-04 · PARENT: T TERMINAL
Cell Grid, Proportional Type
EUREKA
Dissection

"Terminal" is usually treated as one thing, but it is two independent variables wearing a trench coat: a monospaced font and a fixed character cell grid. Every terminal has both, so nobody asks which one carries the feeling.

Mutation

Keep the cell grid — every line locked to a 26 px vertical rhythm, every element snapped to it — but set the text in a proportional humanist sans.

Result

The grid carries it, not the font. The specimen still reads as precise, systematic and machine-adjacent while gaining the readability of proportional type. This is the best of the ten: it recovers roughly 12% horizontal density over monospace and keeps the terminal's authority. Worth promoting out of the lab.

EXP-05 · PARENT: C BENTO
Voronoi Bento
FAILED
Dissection

Bento is tiles of unequal size, uniform radius, even gutters — and, unexamined, rectangles. The rectangle looks like the one arbitrary choice in the set.

Mutation

Replace rectangular tiles with an irregular Voronoi-style partition, keeping every other variable fixed.

Result

Immediate, total failure — visible in the specimen, where text runs straight off the diagonals. Line boxes are always rectangular, so text inside a non-rectangular container either clips or must be manually inset until the container's shape is irrelevant. Finding: bento's rectangles are not an aesthetic preference, they are a text-layout requirement. This mutation is only available to tile sets that hold no prose.

Information density is the point of the bento layout, and this cell can no longer deliver it.
Text clips against the diagonal because a line box cannot follow a slanted edge.
Padding it inward until it fits means the shape stops mattering at all.
Every tile needs bespoke insets. The system stops being a system.
EXP-06 · PARENT: J AURORA
Ambient Data Aurora
EUREKA
Dissection

Aurora's mesh gradient animates on a timer. Its hue, spread and drift are decorative — they carry no information, they just move.

Mutation

Cut the timer. Bind hue and spread to a live value instead, and let the ambient background double as a peripheral gauge.

Result

The most useful discovery of the ten. Decorative motion is a wasted channel; the same pixels can report state without adding a single UI element. Drag the slider — the room changes colour before you consciously read the number. Caveat: it must never be the only channel, since hue alone fails colour-vision and WCAG 1.4.1, which is why the numeric readout stays.

EXP-07 · PARENT: G MAXIMALISM
Monochrome Maximalism
EUREKA
Dissection

Maximalism bundles density, overlap, rotation and clashing hue. Clashing hue is assumed to be the source of the energy.

Mutation

Hold density, overlap and rotation at maximum. Delete hue variety entirely — every layer a tint or shade of a single red.

Result

It stays loud. Hue clash was never the engine — density and rotation were. And the mutation is strictly better behaved: with one hue, brand colour survives contact with maximalism, and text on top remains legible because the tonal range is now controlled. This is the version that could actually ship.

EXP-08 · PARENT: 31 TRUE SKEUOMORPHISM
The Impossible Material
PARTIAL
Dissection

Skeuomorphism works because it obeys physics the user already knows: one light source, consistent bevels, a cast shadow opposite the light.

Mutation

Keep the rendering quality, break the physics. The body is lit from the top-left, the inner bevel from the bottom-right, and the cast shadow falls toward the first light instead of away from it.

Result

Genuinely unsettling — it reads as an object that cannot exist, which is exactly right for games, album art and title sequences. But it destroys the reason skeuomorphism exists: a user cannot infer "press me" from a surface whose lighting they cannot parse. Expressive win, affordance loss. Decoration only, never a control.

EXP-09 · PARENT: 34 ACCESSIBILITY-FIRST
1-Bit
EUREKA
Dissection

Accessibility-first treats contrast as a floor to clear — 4.5:1, 7:1 — and then spends the remaining budget on an aesthetic.

Mutation

Treat contrast as a ceiling to reach instead. Two colours only, pure #000 and #fff, a permanent 21:1. No greys are permitted, so tone must be produced by ordered dithering — the same trick 1-bit displays used.

Result

The happiest accident here: the most accessible palette that can exist turns out to be a beloved aesthetic already (Playdate, Obra Dinn, Mac System 1). Maximum accessibility and strong visual identity are not in tension — the assumed trade-off between them is an artefact of reaching for grey. The canvas shows a smooth gradient rendered in two colours via a 4×4 Bayer matrix.

Loud Calm
PARTIAL
Dissection

EXP-07 removed hue from maximalism and it stayed loud. That leaves two suspects for the energy: density and contrast. This experiment separates them.

Mutation

Identical layer count, overlap and rotation to EXP-07 — the exact same generator — but the entire composition compressed into roughly an 8% tonal band, the palette of Concept Q.

Result

It goes completely quiet. Compare it against EXP-07 directly above: same density, same chaos, no volume at all. Taken together the two experiments answer the question — loudness is carried by contrast, not by hue and not by density. As a result this makes an excellent texture or background layer, and a poor foreground: there is nowhere left to put emphasis, because emphasis is spent.

Log — what the voyage returned
4 EUREKA · 4 PARTIAL · 2 FAILED

Shadow is a lighting model, not a decoration (EXP-03). Offset and blur must agree. Most "hybrid" style mashups die here.

Some constraints are structural, not stylistic (EXP-05). Bento's rectangles look like taste and are actually a requirement of how line boxes work. Worth asking of any variable before mutating it.

Loudness is contrast (EXP-07 + EXP-10). Removing hue left maximalism loud; removing contrast silenced it while every other variable held. Two experiments, one answer.

The famous ingredient is often the wrong one (EXP-04). Terminals feel precise because of the cell grid, not the monospaced font — so the readability penalty everyone pays for that feeling is optional.

An assumed trade-off can just be false (EXP-09). Maximum accessibility was supposed to cost identity. At 1 bit it buys one.
